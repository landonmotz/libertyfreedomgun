Local resident Mark Gable confirmed Tuesday that the standard-grade Kohler Wellworth toilet in his apartment’s master bathroom has officially taken up smoking. The fixture, which has served the household for six years, reportedly consumes roughly half a pack of filtered menthols every twenty-four hours.

According to Gable, the habit began three weeks ago when he noticed a faint scent of tobacco lingering near the base of the pedestal. Initially dismissing the smell as a neighbor’s habit traveling through the air vents, Gable soon discovered a discarded cigarette butt floating in the bowl despite living alone.

"It started out social, just one or two on the weekends when I had friends over for drinks," Gable said while gesturing toward the ceramic tank, which currently houses a small plastic ashtray. "Now, I walk in there at 7:00 a.m. and the whole room is just a thick cloud. It doesn’t even wait for me to leave anymore."

## A Costly Habit For Porcelain

Health experts and plumbing consultants remain baffled by the development. Dr. Arnie Sloss, a researcher at the Institute for Residential Anomalies, noted that while some fixtures develop rust or lime scale, a chemical dependency on nicotine is statistically unprecedented. Sloss believes the toilet may be reacting to the high-stress environment of a single-bedroom apartment.

> "I just thought it was some weird condensation until I found the ashes in the tank."

The habit has already begun to take a toll on the toilet's appearance. The once-pristine white porcelain is developing a yellowish hue, and the water in the bowl has taken on a brownish tint that Gable insists is unrelated to its primary function. Despite the health risks, the toilet shows no signs of slowing down, often flickering its handle in a way that suggests it is craving a light.

At press time, Gable was reportedly looking for a nicotine patch large enough to fit over the flush valve.
